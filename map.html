Brighter means more people. The heat layer answers a different question from the bubble map: not “which countries spend most” but “where the people are”. Each point is weighted by national population, normalised to the largest country in the set.

Reading the two maps together

The bubble map is drawn from the capital city coordinates supplied by the World Bank, so every point stands in for a whole country — it is a symbol map, not a survey. The pattern it shows is that health spending and longevity are broadly spread across Europe, North America and East Asia, while low-spending countries sit along a band through sub-Saharan Africa and South Asia.

The heat map is built from exactly the same CSV file, which is the point worth noticing: a single dataset can be made to argue two completely different things depending on how it is drawn.
